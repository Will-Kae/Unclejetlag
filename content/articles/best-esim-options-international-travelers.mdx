A travel eSIM is the easiest way to be online the moment you land. It's also easy to get wrong. The usual problems are a phone that's locked to its carrier, or a plan that doesn't match how you travel.

**This guide covers:**

- how eSIMs compare with local SIMs and roaming
- what to check before you buy
- how the best-known providers differ, based on their published plan structures and independent 2026 testing

**Local SIMs often win on price.** In South Africa, for example, a monthly bundle of about 5 GB costs roughly R100–170. In Georgia, 1 GB of Magti data is about ₾5. The trade-off is registration paperwork and queues at the airport.

## Bundles vs "unlimited": the one thing to understand

- **GB bundles** (e.g. 5 GB for 15 days) run at full speed until the data runs out. You can then top up.
- **"Unlimited" plans** usually have a **daily fair-use threshold of about 3–5 GB**. After that, speeds drop to below 1 Mbps until the next day. That's fine for maps and messaging, but painful for video calls, uploads or tethering a laptop.

If you work remotely or hotspot a laptop, a **large GB bundle is usually more reliable** than "unlimited".

## How to choose a travel eSIM

1. **Compatibility and unlock status.** Your phone must support eSIM and be carrier-unlocked.
2. **Single country or regional plan.** Visiting three countries? A regional plan can beat three single-country plans.
3. **Allowance and validity.** Match both to your trip. A 10 GB plan that expires after 7 days is useless on a 14-day trip.
4. **Local network partner.** Check which network(s) the plan uses. In rural areas, a partner with better coverage matters more than headline speed.
5. **Hotspot policy.** Check tethering is allowed and not separately capped.
6. **Top-ups.** Can you add data in the app without reinstalling?
7. **Data-only or a phone number.** Most travel eSIMs are data-only. Internet calling works in most places, but **not in the UAE**, where WhatsApp and FaceTime voice and video calls are generally blocked on mobile networks.

**Quick rules of thumb:**

- **Weekend city break, light use:** a small GB bundle from any major provider.
- **Multi-country trip:** a regional GB plan. Airalo's range is the widest.
- **You'll stream or video call every day:** a large GB bundle, or a local SIM, rather than "unlimited".
- **Japan or South Korea with heavy data use:** Ubigi has tested well on 5G.
- **Stays of 2+ weeks in one country:** a local SIM is almost always cheaper.

## Setup checklist

- Confirm eSIM support and unlock status
- Install on Wi-Fi before you leave, and label the line "Travel"
- Set it as your data line, and keep your home SIM for calls and SMS if needed
- Turn off data roaming on your home line, or you may still be charged
- Screenshot the QR code and activation details in case you need to reinstall
- On arrival, switch on data roaming **for the eSIM line**. Many travel eSIMs technically roam on a local partner network.
