Whether an airline owes you money for a delay depends on two things: **where the flight departs from** and **which airline operates it**. The EU and UK pay fixed compensation of up to €600 or £520. Canada pays up to $1,000 for delays the airline caused. The US only guarantees a refund, not compensation.

<Callout type="info">
  These are the rules as published by each regulator on 9 October 2026. This is general information, not legal advice. Always check the regulator's page before you claim.
</Callout>

## EU: Regulation 261/2004

**Who is covered.** Every flight within the EU, every flight departing the EU on any airline, and flights arriving in the EU from outside **only if an EU airline operates them**. A Johannesburg to Frankfurt flight on Lufthansa is covered. The same route on a non-EU airline is not, but its return leg from Frankfurt is.

**How much.** Compensation depends on distance, and applies when you reach your final destination **3 hours or more late**:

- **€250** for flights of 1,500 km or less
- **€400** for flights within the EU over 1,500 km, and other flights of 1,500 to 3,500 km
- **€600** for flights over 3,500 km. On those flights to or from outside the EU, a delay of 3 to 4 hours pays **€300**, and the full €600 needs 4 hours or more

If the airline reroutes you and you arrive within 2, 3 or 4 hours of schedule (depending on distance), compensation is cut by half.

**When the airline doesn't pay.** Extraordinary circumstances are excluded: air traffic management decisions, political instability, bad weather and security risks. Most technical problems and strikes by the airline's own staff usually **do** count against the airline, and **the airline has to prove** the cause.

**While you wait.** Once a delay passes 2, 3 or 4 hours (depending on distance), the airline must give you meals and refreshments. If the new departure is the next day, it must also provide a hotel and transport to it. If the departure is delayed **5 hours or more**, you can choose a refund instead of flying.

<Callout type="tip">
  **The EU rules are changing, but not yet.** The Council of the EU gave final clearance to a reform on 13 July 2026. It keeps the 3-hour trigger and the €250/€400/€600 amounts. It also adds a duty to tell you your rights and a 30-day deadline to pay or explain a refusal. It only takes effect 12 months and 20 days after publication in the EU's Official Journal, so the current rules apply for the rest of 2026.
</Callout>

## UK: UK261

The UK kept the EU system after Brexit, with amounts in pounds. It covers flights **departing the UK** on any airline, flights **arriving in the UK** on a UK or EU airline, and flights arriving in the EU on a UK airline.

- **£220** for flights under 1,500 km
- **£350** for 1,500 to 3,500 km
- **£520** over 3,500 km, or **£260** if you arrive 3 to 4 hours late

For cancellations, compensation may be due if the airline told you **less than 14 days** before departure, and you can choose a refund or an alternative flight. With a delay of more than 5 hours you can take a refund instead of flying. The CAA is clear that **compensation isn't automatic: you have to claim it**.

## United States: refunds, not compensation

US law requires a **refund**, not extra compensation. You are entitled to an automatic refund if your flight is **cancelled or significantly changed**. That means it departs early or arrives late by **3 hours or more on a domestic flight, or 6 hours or more on an international one**, and you choose not to travel. Refunds are due within **7 business days** for card payments and **20 days** for other methods.

There is **no federal compensation for delays**. In November 2025 the Department of Transportation withdrew its plan to require cash compensation, meals and hotels, saying the law doesn't give it that power. What you can get is whatever the airline itself promises for delays it caused. DOT publishes those promises on its [cancellation and delay dashboard](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-cancellation-delay-dashboard) and says it will hold airlines to them.

From 19 October 2026, fewer US disruptions count as the airline's fault. [Read our update on the 10 reclassified delay causes](/guides/us-dot-narrows-controllable-flight-delay-causes).

## Canada: APPR

Canada pays compensation for delays that are **within the airline's control and not required for safety**. For large airlines:

- **$400** for a 3 to 6 hour delay
- **$700** for 6 to 9 hours
- **$1,000** for 9 hours or more

Small airlines pay $125, $250 or $500. Proposed changes published in December 2024 would simplify these categories, but as of October 2026 the Canadian Transportation Agency describes them as still being worked on.

## South Africa: refunds for overbooking, no delay payout

We found no South African law that pays fixed compensation for flight delays. The **Consumer Protection Act** does ban overbooking: if you're not carried because a flight was oversold, you're entitled to a refund with interest and can claim damages. For delays, your rights come from the airline's own terms and from EU, UK or other rules if your route falls under them.

## How to claim

1. **Write it all down at the airport.** Note the scheduled and actual arrival time at your **final destination**, and the reason the airline gives. Photograph departure boards and keep boarding passes.
2. **Keep receipts** for meals, transport and hotels if the airline didn't provide them.
3. **Claim directly with the airline** using its online form. Quote the rule (for example "EU Regulation 261/2004"), the flight number, date and the delay.
4. **If the airline refuses or ignores you,** escalate to the national enforcement body or regulator for that country: the CAA in the UK, the Canadian Transportation Agency, or the DOT for US complaints.

You don't need a claims company. They take a share of your payout for filling in the same form.

## Before your next flight

The best protection is a route that falls under the strongest rules. When two itineraries cost about the same, an EU or UK airline on a long-haul route into Europe gives you EU261 or UK261 cover in both directions.
