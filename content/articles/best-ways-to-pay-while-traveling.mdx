The best way to pay abroad isn't one card. It's a **system**: a main method, a backup on a different rail, and a little cash, so that no single failure ruins your day.

## The three-layer setup

1. **Primary:** a card with no (or low) foreign transaction fees, added to your phone wallet.
2. **Backup:** a second card on the *other* network (Visa ↔ Mastercard), from a different bank, stored separately.
3. **Cash:** a small amount of local currency for the airport transfer, tips and places that don't take cards.

## What each option really costs

- **No-fee card, paying in local currency:** close to the Visa or Mastercard network rate. Check it with the [Visa](https://www.visa.co.uk/support/consumer/travel-support/exchange-rate-calculator.html) or [Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/personal/get-support/currency-exchange-rate-converter.html) calculators.
- **Card with a foreign transaction fee:** the network rate plus your bank's percentage, often 2–3% per purchase.
- **Accepting "pay in your home currency" (DCC):** the merchant provider's rate and markup. In the EU, the markup must be shown as a percentage before you pay.
- **Airport cash counter:** commonly around 14% above reference rates, according to NerdWallet's September 2026 figures.

## A note for travellers from Africa

Many travellers from African countries face extra friction that most travel-money guides ignore:

- **International transactions disabled by default** on some local cards.
- **Caps on foreign spending or withdrawals** on local-currency cards in some markets.
- **Fintech availability** varies: a multi-currency product popular in Europe may not accept residents of your country.

## Security basics

- Use ATMs inside banks or busy areas.
- Turn on transaction notifications.
- Know how to freeze each card from its app.
- Keep the backup card in a different bag from your main wallet.
- Save your bank's international helpline number offline.
- Choose **credit over debit** for hotel and car-hire deposits where you can. Holds on a debit card lock up your own money until they're released.
