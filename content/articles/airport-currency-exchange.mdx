Airport exchange counters sell one thing: **convenience at the moment you have the fewest alternatives.** They're rarely a scam. They're just priced for a captive audience.

## What the numbers say

NerdWallet's September 2026 update says:

- airport exchanges commonly charge **around 14% above the IMF reference rate**
- some premiums it found were **above 17%**
- Bank of America averaged **roughly 6%**, less than half the airport premium

On US$500, a 14% premium costs about **US$70** before any fees. A no-foreign-fee card at the mid-market rate costs close to nothing.

## Why the rates are usually worse

- **Location costs:** airport rents are high, and that's recovered through the spread.
- **Captive customers:** you've just landed, you need a taxi, and there's one counter in sight.
- **"No commission" signage:** the cost is in the rate, not a fee.

## "Buy back" and "sell" rates

Counters display two prices: the rate at which they **sell** you foreign currency and the rate at which they **buy** it back. The gap between them is their margin. A wide gap means you lose twice if you change money on the way out and back again.

## When it still makes sense

- The destination is cash-heavy and you need money for transport immediately.
- ATMs at the airport are out of order or don't accept your card.
- You're exchanging a **small amount**, enough to get to your accommodation.

## Better options on arrival

1. **An airport bank ATM**, withdrawing in local currency and declining any conversion offer. Check the operator fee shown on screen before you confirm.
2. **A card or phone wallet** for the taxi or train if accepted.
3. **Exchange a small amount**, then use city exchange offices or ATMs once you can compare.

<Callout type="tip">
  Watch the *buy-back* rate too. If you bring leftover cash home, you'll often lose again on the way back. Spend coins before leaving: they're usually impossible to exchange.
</Callout>

## Before you fly: a 5-minute plan

1. Check the mid-market rate and write it down.
2. Know which card you'll use at the airport ATM and that international use is on.
3. Save the route and price of your airport transfer, and whether it takes cards.
4. If you must buy cash before you go, order it from your bank in advance. It's usually cheaper than buying at the departures counter.
