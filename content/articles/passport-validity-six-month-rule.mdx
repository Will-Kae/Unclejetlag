Your passport has to do more than be valid on the day you fly. **Many countries require it to stay valid for a set period after you arrive or leave.** Airlines enforce this at check-in, before a border officer ever sees you.

## Why "six months" isn't one universal rule

"Six-month rule" is shorthand for the idea that your passport must keep plenty of validity. **The actual rules vary a lot.** Here are three real examples:

| Destination | Validity rule | Other document rules |
|---|---|---|
| Schengen Area (29 European countries) | At least **3 months after you intend to leave** | Passport must have been **issued within the last 10 years** |
| United Arab Emirates (Dubai visa) | At least **6 months** | None |
| South Africa | At least **30 days after you leave** | At least **2 consecutive completely blank visa pages** |

Sources: EU "Your Europe", GDRFA Dubai, U.S. Department of State.

## The Schengen 10-year trap

The EU rule has two parts:

- valid for **3 months after your planned departure**
- **issued within the previous 10 years** on the day you enter

Some countries have extended old passports with stamps, or issue passports valid for more than 10 years. Holders of those passports can be refused boarding even though the expiry date looks fine.

<Callout type="info">
  The Schengen area's **Entry/Exit System (EES)** became fully operational on **10 April 2026**. It records non-EU travellers' fingerprints and facial images at the border instead of stamping passports. Expect longer first-time border checks.
</Callout>

## Why airlines care

If you're refused entry:

- the airline can be fined
- it has to fly you back at its own cost

So check-in agents use the same databases border systems use (such as IATA's Timatic) and **won't board you if your passport fails the rule**. "The border might let me in" isn't an argument that works at a check-in desk.

## Other passport problems that stop travel

- **Not enough blank pages** for stamps or visas. South Africa needs two consecutive blank pages.
- **Damage:** water damage, torn or missing pages, a separated cover or a damaged photo page.
- **Name mismatch** between your passport and ticket, including a missing middle name on some airlines.
- **Reported lost or stolen**, even if you later found it. Once reported, the passport is cancelled.
- **A visa in an old passport:** some countries let you travel with both passports, others require a transfer. Check before you fly.

## A simple rule of thumb

- **Renew when you have about 9–12 months left** if you travel internationally. That covers six-month rules, visa processing times and multi-country trips.
- **Check transit countries too.** Some apply their own rules even if you don't leave the airport.
- **Check the official source** for your destination before booking, and again a week before you fly.

<Callout type="tip">
  Applying for a visa? Many visas need a passport with plenty of validity remaining. A new passport can also mean transferring or re-applying for a visa, so time your renewal before any visa applications.
</Callout>
