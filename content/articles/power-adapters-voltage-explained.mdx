Plugging in abroad involves **two separate problems**:

- **The shape of the plug.** An adapter fixes this.
- **The voltage behind the socket.** Only a voltage converter, or a device built for both voltages, fixes this.

Most travellers only ever need an adapter. The trick is knowing when you don't.

## Adapter vs converter

- **Plug adapter:** changes the pin shape so your plug fits. It doesn't change the electricity. It's small and cheap, and it's all you need for most modern electronics.
- **Voltage converter or transformer:** changes the voltage, for example 230V down to 120V. You only need one for *single-voltage* devices. Converters are heavy, and cheap ones can damage electronics.

## Read the label first

Look for this small print on your charger:

**INPUT: 100–240V ~ 50/60Hz**

That means it works on both 120V systems (North America) and 230V systems (most of the world). Phone chargers, laptop bricks, camera chargers and most electric toothbrush chargers are dual-voltage.

**The usual exceptions are heating appliances:** hair dryers, straighteners, travel kettles and some shavers. A 120V-only hair dryer plugged into 230V can burn out in seconds. Buy a dual-voltage travel model, or use the one at your hotel.

<Callout type="warning" title="The South Africa trap">
  Many "universal" travel adapters don't include South Africa's large round-pin **Type M**. Check that the product explicitly lists Type M or South Africa, or buy one on arrival. They're sold at airport shops and supermarkets.
</Callout>

## Travelling from 230V to 120V (or the other way)

- **Dual-voltage devices:** just use an adapter.
- **Single-voltage devices:** buy a converter rated above the device's wattage, or leave the device at home.
- **The 50Hz/60Hz difference** rarely matters for chargers. It can affect motorised devices and some clocks.

## Power banks and airline rules

The FAA's baseline rules, which most airlines follow or tighten, are:

- **Spare lithium batteries and power banks go in carry-on only**, never in checked bags.
- **The limit is 100 Wh per battery.** With airline approval, you can carry up to two spare batteries of 101–160 Wh.
- **Protect battery terminals** from short circuits. Use a case, or tape over the contacts.

Several airlines also ban **using** power banks in flight or limit how many you can carry, so check your airline's page before you fly.

## Smart packing

- **One good multi-region adapter plus a multi-port USB-C charger** beats a bag of adapters.
- **GaN chargers** (65–100W) can charge a laptop, phone and watch from one socket.
- **A short multi-socket extension lead** lets one adapter power everything. Check it's rated for the local voltage.
- **Label your adapter with your name.** Hotel sockets are where adapters go to die.
