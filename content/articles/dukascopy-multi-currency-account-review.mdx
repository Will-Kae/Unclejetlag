**The short version:** Dukascopy Bank lets most non-Americans open a real Swiss bank account from home, with IBANs in 24 currencies, by video call. It's useful as a **second, multi-currency account** for receiving money from abroad, holding foreign currency and paying on the road. It's **not** a cheap everyday current account, and its cards are prepaid, not debit or credit.

## What Dukascopy Bank is

Dukascopy Bank SA is a Swiss bank based in Geneva, **regulated by FINMA** (Switzerland's financial regulator) and a member of the Swiss Bankers Association. It's best known as a forex and trading platform. The product reviewed here is its **Multi-Currency Account (MCA)**: an ordinary bank account, not a trading account.

## Who can open one

- **Age:** 14 and over, with full account features for adults aged 18 and over.
- **Where you live:** Dukascopy says the account can be opened worldwide **except** by residents of a listed set of countries and regions. The list includes the **United States**, **Japan**, the **Russian Federation**, Belarus, Iran, Cuba, North Korea, Syria, Venezuela, Myanmar, Afghanistan, Sudan, South Sudan, Yemen and several disputed regions.
- **ID:** a document with a machine-readable zone (MRZ) and visible security features. In practice, that means a passport.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check before you apply">
  Dukascopy's trading website separately states that it isn't intended to solicit residents of Belgium, Israel, Russia, Turkey, Canada and the UK. Eligibility rules can change, so check the current list on Dukascopy's FAQ for your country of residence before you start.
</Callout>

## How opening works

1. Apply online or in the **Dukascopy Bank** app (iOS and Android).
2. Complete a **video identification** call with a bank employee. Swiss law accepts this in place of a branch visit.
3. You may be asked for an **initial deposit of EUR 40** by card or wire. It isn't a fee: the money is available in your account once it's open.

<Callout type="tip" title="Avoid the dormancy charge">
  The biggest fee is easy to avoid. It applies when the bank **loses contact with you**, not simply because you rarely use the account. Keep your email, phone and address up to date, and log in occasionally.
</Callout>

## Cards

Dukascopy offers **prepaid** Visa and Mastercard cards, both physical and virtual. You top them up from your Multi-Currency Account. Available cards include the Dukascopy card by Cornèrcard, the Swiss Bankers card, and cards from Walletto, CBH and Moorwand. **Which ones you can get depends on where you live.**

Card spending in a foreign currency carries a **1.5% to 2% FX mark-up**. That's fine for occasional use, but more than the best no-foreign-fee cards charge. For how that compares, read [how foreign exchange fees work](/money/how-foreign-exchange-fees-work).

## Deposit protection

Dukascopy states that deposits are **insured up to CHF 100,000 per client** under Swiss legislation.

## Who it suits

- **Frequent travellers and remote workers** who get paid, or pay, in several currencies and want one place to hold them.
- **Travellers from countries where local cards are hard to use abroad.** A Swiss account with a prepaid card is a useful **backup on a different rail**. See [the three-layer payment setup](/money/best-ways-to-pay-while-traveling).
- **People who want a foreign-currency account** in a well-regulated jurisdiction, alongside their main bank.

**It's less suited to** people looking for the cheapest possible card for everyday spending abroad, and to anyone who needs a credit card.

<Callout type="info" title="South African and other exchange-controlled residents">
  If your home country has exchange controls, as South Africa does, moving money into a foreign account is governed by your central bank's rules and allowances. Ask your bank or a registered tax practitioner before you transfer funds offshore. This is general information, not financial advice.
</Callout>
