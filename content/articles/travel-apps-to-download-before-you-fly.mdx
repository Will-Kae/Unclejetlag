Your phone is your map, wallet, ticket, translator and emergency contact list. The trick is to set it up **before** you're standing in an arrivals hall on airport Wi-Fi.

## The essential categories

| Category | Why | Set up before you fly |
|---|---|---|
| Offline maps | Navigation without data | Download the city/region map |
| Ride-hailing / local transport | Getting from the airport safely | Check which apps operate at your destination; add a payment method |
| Translation | Menus, signs, conversations | Download the offline language pack |
| Banking | Freezing cards, limits, alerts | Enable international use and notifications |
| Airline | Boarding passes, delay alerts | Add your booking; enable notifications |
| Messaging | Keeping in touch over data | Make sure backups are on |
| Documents | Passport scans, bookings, insurance | Save offline copies in an encrypted folder |
| Currency converter ([QeFX](https://converter.qefxmoney.com), our founder's tool) | Sense-checking prices | Set Travel Mode to your home and destination currencies |

<Callout type="tip">
  Download maps and translation packs at home on Wi-Fi. Airport Wi-Fi is slow, often requires registration, and always seems to run out the moment you need it.
</Callout>

## Setting up the two most important ones

**Offline maps (Google Maps)**

- Search for your destination city, open its place page, and choose **Download offline map**.
- Downloaded areas **expire**. Google updates them over Wi-Fi when they're within 15 days of expiry, so refresh them before long trips.
- Offline, **transit and cycling directions aren't available**, and driving has no live traffic.
- Some countries and regions can't be downloaded at all. Check before you rely on it.

**Offline translation (Google Translate)**

- Download the language packs for your destination. Google notes the files are small.
- For some languages, you can also point your camera at a sign or menu for instant translation.

**Your eSIM**

- Install it on Wi-Fi before you fly. Apple's guide covers QR-code and carrier setup on iPhone.

## Privacy on the road

Public Wi-Fi is convenient but not private. Stick to HTTPS sites and trusted apps, keep your phone's software updated, and consider a reputable VPN on open networks. Lock screens with a PIN, not just face or fingerprint, before crossing borders or going out at night.

## Don't forget

- Your eSIM, installed and labelled ([how to choose one](/travel-tech/best-esim-options-international-travelers))
- Emergency numbers for your destination saved offline (see our [destination guides](/destinations))
- A power bank that complies with airline rules: carry-on only, and 100 Wh or less without airline approval (FAA)
