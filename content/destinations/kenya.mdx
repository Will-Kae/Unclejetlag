## Overview

Kenya is the classic East African safari country: the Maasai Mara, Amboseli beneath Kilimanjaro, Tsavo and Samburu, plus a long Indian Ocean coast with Swahili towns and beaches. Nairobi is a regional business and transport hub with a national park on its doorstep.

Safari prices and availability depend heavily on season, so book camps and conservancies early for July to October. Entry is now managed through an online authorisation, which makes it essential to apply before you fly.

## Entry requirements

Kenya requires an **Electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA)** for most visitors, applied for online before departure. The system replaced the earlier e-visa and removed visa fees for many nationalities, but rules differ by passport. Citizens of the East African Community are exempt.

Apply early, use only the official government portal and keep a printed copy of your approval. Look-alike sites charge extra fees for the same form. A yellow fever certificate may be requested if you arrive from a country with transmission.

## Best time to visit

July to October is peak season for the wildebeest migration in the Maasai Mara, with high demand and prices. January to February is hot and dry and good for wildlife, while the long rains (roughly March to May) and short rains (roughly November) cause the quietest period with lower rates. The coast is hottest between January and March.

## Money

The currency is the **Kenyan shilling (KES)**. Cards work in Nairobi hotels, malls, safari lodges and larger restaurants. **M-Pesa** mobile money is how most locals pay, including at small shops and for transport, while visitors usually rely on cards and cash. ATMs are widely available in cities, and safari camps often add tourism levies, so ask what is included before you book. Tipping guides and camp staff is customary.

For everyday spending, compare [the best ways to pay while travelling](/money/best-ways-to-pay-while-traveling) and always choose the local currency when a terminal offers to convert ([why](/money/pay-in-local-currency-or-home-currency)).

## Mobile data and eSIMs

Safaricom and Airtel sell SIM cards at the airport and in shops, with passport registration. Coverage is good in Nairobi and along main roads and patchy in parks and conservancies. A travel eSIM is the simplest option for arrival.

A travel eSIM avoids hunting for a SIM shop after a long flight. Use our [eSIM Finder](/esim) to estimate how much data you need.

## Getting around

Nairobi traffic is heavy, so allow plenty of time for airport transfers. Ride-hailing apps are widely used, and domestic flights connect Nairobi with the Mara, Amboseli and the coast. A comfortable overland option is the Standard Gauge Railway between Nairobi and Mombasa. Kenya drives on the **left**. Safari roads can be rough, and a driver-guide is usually better than self-driving.

## Safety and health

Take usual city precautions in Nairobi: keep phones and bags out of sight, avoid walking at night and use trusted transport. Government advice warns against travel to some border regions, so check it before you plan routes. Malaria is present in many areas, so speak to a travel clinic. Follow your guide's instructions around wildlife.

## Things to do

- Maasai Mara for the migration and big cats
- Amboseli for elephants with Kilimanjaro behind
- Nairobi National Park and the Giraffe Centre
- Diani Beach and the Lamu archipelago on the coast
- Mount Kenya and Hell's Gate for hiking

## Airports

**Jomo Kenyatta International (NBO)** is the main hub, with Mombasa's Moi International (MBA) for the coast. Many safari flights depart from Nairobi's Wilson Airport instead, so check which airport your onward flight uses.

## Local customs

Greet people with a handshake and a few words of Swahili, such as *jambo* or *asante*. Dress modestly in towns and mosques, ask before photographing people, and note that camouflage clothing is restricted. Bargaining is normal in markets.

## Emergency numbers

Police, fire and ambulance: **999**. Calls to **112** also work from mobile phones.
