## Overview

Qatar is a small, wealthy Gulf state built around Doha, with a striking skyline, Souq Waqif's traditional market, world-class museums and desert dunes within an hour's drive. Hamad International Airport makes it a major connecting hub between Africa, Europe, Asia and the Americas, and many travellers use it for a stopover.

Plan for heat, dress and conduct rules that are more conservative than in many global cities, and enjoy easy transport and clean, safe streets.

## Entry requirements

Entry rules depend on your passport. Many nationalities can enter **visa-free** or with a **visa on arrival** for a limited stay, and others need to apply in advance. Qatar uses the official **Hayya platform** for some entry-related processes, so check Visit Qatar and Hayya for the current requirements.

Passports typically need several months of validity. Transit passengers should check whether a visa is needed to leave the airport during a long layover.

## Best time to visit

November to April is the best time to visit, with warm days and cooler evenings. Summer is extremely hot and humid, so most activities move indoors or to evenings. Ramadan changes opening hours, with restaurants quiet by day and busy after sunset.

## Money

The currency is the **Qatari riyal (QAR)**, which is pegged to the US dollar. Cards and contactless are accepted almost everywhere, and ATMs are common. Service charges are often included, and a tip of around ten percent is appreciated for good service. Souq shopping often involves polite bargaining.

For everyday spending, compare [the best ways to pay while travelling](/money/best-ways-to-pay-while-traveling) and always choose the local currency when a terminal offers to convert ([why](/money/pay-in-local-currency-or-home-currency)).

## Mobile data and eSIMs

Ooredoo and Vodafone Qatar sell tourist SIMs at the airport and in shops, with passport registration. A travel eSIM is straightforward. Be aware that some voice-over-internet services are restricted.

A travel eSIM avoids hunting for a SIM shop after a long flight. Use our [eSIM Finder](/esim) to estimate how much data you need.

## Getting around

The **Doha Metro** and tram network are modern and cheap, and they serve the airport. Taxis are metered, and ride-hailing apps work in Doha. Car hire is common, but traffic rules are strictly enforced, and fines are heavy. Qatar drives on the **right**.

## Safety and health

Qatar is very safe, with low crime. Observe local laws: alcohol is restricted to licensed venues, drugs carry severe penalties, and public displays of affection are discouraged. Dress modestly in public places and malls, with shoulders and knees covered. Avoid photographing government buildings or local people without permission. Heat illness is a real risk in summer.

## Things to do

- Souq Waqif's alleys, cafés and falconry stalls
- The Museum of Islamic Art and the National Museum of Qatar
- Katara Cultural Village and the Pearl Qatar
- The desert and Khor Al Adaid, the Inland Sea, on a guided trip
- Doha's Corniche at sunset

## Airports

**Hamad International (DOH)** is a large, modern hub with a Metro link to the city. A stopover tour can be arranged through Qatar Airways, and layover visa rules differ, so check before leaving the terminal.

## Local customs

Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered, particularly for women and at religious sites. Greet people politely, and use your right hand to give and receive. Do not eat, drink or smoke in public during Ramadan daylight hours. Ask before photographing people.

## Emergency numbers

Police, fire and ambulance: **999**.
