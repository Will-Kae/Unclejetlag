## Overview

Peru is about Machu Picchu and the Sacred Valley, but also Lima's food scene, the colonial city of Arequipa, Lake Titicaca, the Colca Canyon and the Amazon basin. It is one of South America's top destinations for culture, hiking and food.

The main planning challenges are advance bookings for Machu Picchu and the Inca Trail, the altitude in the Andes and travel logistics in rainy season. Plan time to acclimatise before big treks.

## Entry requirements

Many nationalities can enter Peru as tourists **without a visa** for a limited stay set by the border officer, and others need a visa from a Peruvian consulate. Check the Peruvian authorities' guidance for your passport. Keep your passport and the immigration record safe, as you may be asked for them when leaving.

Carry proof of onward travel and accommodation. A **yellow fever vaccination** is recommended if you visit parts of the Amazon basin, so speak to a travel clinic.

## Best time to visit

May to September is the dry season in the Andes, with clear days and cold nights, and the best time for trekking. October to April is wetter, and the Inca Trail closes in February for maintenance. The coast around Lima is grey and cool for much of the year, and best in the southern summer from December to April.

## Money

The currency is the **Peruvian sol (PEN)**. Cards are accepted in hotels, restaurants and shops in cities and tourist areas, but carry cash for markets, taxis, rural areas and small purchases. US dollars are accepted at some tourist businesses but often at poor rates, and notes must usually be clean and undamaged. Use ATMs inside banks, and tip around ten percent in restaurants if service is not included.

For everyday spending, compare [the best ways to pay while travelling](/money/best-ways-to-pay-while-traveling) and always choose the local currency when a terminal offers to convert ([why](/money/pay-in-local-currency-or-home-currency)).

## Mobile data and eSIMs

Claro, Movistar and Entel sell SIMs, and a travel eSIM works in cities and tourist areas. Coverage is limited in remote mountains and the Amazon. Download offline maps before trekking.

A travel eSIM avoids hunting for a SIM shop after a long flight. Use our [eSIM Finder](/esim) to estimate how much data you need.

## Getting around

Domestic flights link Lima with Cusco, Arequipa and other cities, and they save long road trips. Long-distance buses are widely used and have different comfort levels, so choose reputable companies. Trains run to Machu Picchu from Ollantaytambo and Cusco area. Ride-hailing apps work in Lima and Cusco, and registered taxis are best. Peru drives on the **right**.

## Safety and health

Petty theft is common in Lima and tourist spots, so keep bags close and use app-booked or registered taxis, especially at night. Protests and road blockades can disrupt travel, so check local news and have buffer days before flights. Take altitude seriously, and drink bottled or filtered water. Check government advice before you travel.

## Things to do

- Machu Picchu and the Sacred Valley near Cusco
- The Inca Trail or an alternative trek, booked months ahead
- Lima's food scene in Miraflores and Barranco
- Arequipa and the Colca Canyon
- Lake Titicaca and the Amazon region

## Airports

**Lima (LIM)** is the main gateway, and **Cusco (CUZ)** is the base for Machu Picchu. Many flights to Cusco are affected by weather, so keep schedule flexibility.

## Local customs

Greet people politely, and ask before photographing locals, particularly in Andean communities. Coca tea is common for altitude, but coca leaves cannot be taken across most international borders. Bargain politely in markets.
