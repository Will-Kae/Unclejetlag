## Overview

Jamaica is more than all-inclusive resorts: reggae in Kingston, waterfalls and rivers near Ocho Rios, cliffs and sunsets in Negril, the Blue Mountains for coffee and hiking, and Port Antonio's quieter coast. It is a lively island with strong food, music and personality.

Most visitors arrive at Montego Bay. Decide early whether you want a resort-based trip or a more independent one, and plan transfers accordingly.

## Entry requirements

Many nationalities can enter Jamaica **without a visa** for a short stay, while others need to apply for a visa in advance. Check the official immigration agency for your passport. Keep your passport valid for the length of your stay, and carry proof of onward travel.

Check whether an online **immigration and customs declaration** is required before arrival, since requirements can change.

## Best time to visit

Mid-December to April is the dry and sunny peak season, with the highest prices. May to June and November are quieter. Hurricane season runs roughly June to November, with the highest risk in August to October, so check forecasts and consider flexible bookings and travel insurance.

## Money

The currency is the **Jamaican dollar (JMD)**, though **US dollars are widely accepted** in tourist areas. Cards work in hotels, resorts and larger restaurants. Use ATMs inside banks, and keep cash for taxis, markets and smaller venues. Tipping of about 10 to 15 percent is customary where a service charge is not included, so check your bill.

For everyday spending, compare [the best ways to pay while travelling](/money/best-ways-to-pay-while-traveling) and always choose the local currency when a terminal offers to convert ([why](/money/pay-in-local-currency-or-home-currency)).

## Mobile data and eSIMs

Digicel and Flow sell SIMs, and a travel eSIM is simple. Coverage is good on the north coast and in towns and weaker in some mountain areas.

## Getting around

Taxis and pre-arranged transfers are the standard way to get around. Use licensed, registered taxis or hotel-arranged transfers, and note that many tours include transport. Public routes taxis and buses exist but are busy. Jamaica drives on the **left**, and roads can be narrow and winding, so car hire needs care. Allow generous journey times between towns.

## Safety and health

Crime is a concern in parts of Jamaica, so follow your government's advice, stay in well-known areas, use licensed transport and avoid walking at night. Be cautious about unsolicited offers on beaches. Take sun protection, and pay attention to sea conditions. Cannabis is partly decriminalised for personal use, but it remains illegal to carry on flights or across borders.

## Things to do

- Negril's Seven Mile Beach and cliff sunsets
- Dunn's River Falls and Ocho Rios
- The Blue Mountains for coffee and hiking
- Kingston's Bob Marley Museum and music scene
- Port Antonio and the Blue Lagoon

## Airports

**Sangster International (MBJ)** in Montego Bay is the main tourist gateway, and **Norman Manley (KIN)** serves Kingston. Arrange a transfer in advance for the longer drives to Negril and Ocho Rios.

## Local customs

Greet people politely, since a friendly hello is expected. Ask before photographing people. Jamaicans are proud of their culture, so show respect for local music and food. Dress is relaxed at beaches but modest in towns.

## Emergency numbers

Police: **119**. Fire and ambulance: **110**.
